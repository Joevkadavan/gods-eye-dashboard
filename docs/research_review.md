# Research Review, Gaps and Proposed Contribution

**Status:** focused, verifiable literature review for system design. It is not yet a systematic PRISMA review and novelty is not claimed. Paper-reported numbers below are quotations of each paper's own benchmark and must not be compared as if they shared hardware or protocols.

## Candidate research gaps

1. **Offline benchmark metrics vs. deployment evidence.** Papers commonly report frame-level AUC/EER or tracker metrics, while camera operators need event-level false alarms, time-to-alert, event verification burden, processing latency and stale-frame rate under multi-camera load. These are different quantities and are rarely directly comparable.
2. **Resource-aware temporal confirmation under tight CPU limits.** Edge research and anomaly models each address efficiency or temporal signals, but a capstone-relevant question remains how a bounded inference budget affects the quality and timeliness of a *human-reviewable event* (not just per-frame accuracy) under changing scene load. This is a candidate gap, not a claim that no prior method exists.
3. **Privacy and retention are often separate from detector evaluation.** Skeleton/flow representations can reduce use of raw appearance but add model cost and do not eliminate privacy risk. A testable design should minimise video persistence and avoid identity linkage while preserving enough event evidence for human audit.
4. **Cross-camera identity correlation creates scope and risk.** A multi-camera dashboard does not require re-identification. Camera-local temporary tracks can support crowd/traffic statistics and temporal cues without linking the same person across cameras; the costs/benefits should be measured rather than assuming persistent identity is necessary.
5. **Traffic “counts” are not all traffic flow.** Counting visible vehicle detections each frame is not a flow count. A per-camera calibrated virtual line, direction and unique track crossing are needed for vehicles/minute; this prototype currently reports newly appearing vehicle-track rate as a proxy, not validated roadway flow.

## Proposed technical contribution (research hypothesis)

### Budgeted Camera-Local Temporal Evidence (BCLTE)

The proposed, falsifiable contribution is a **resource-budgeted temporal evidence policy** for safety-event triage, rather than an unsubstantiated claim that a bundle of YOLO + tracking + alerts is novel:

- allocate a fixed inference-time budget across independently monitored cameras;
- compute camera-local observable cues (track displacement, count change, object persistence) and maintain a short rolling temporal state;
- require a cue sequence and persistence window before emitting an alert, attach the supporting time window and a transient evidence frame, and expose uncertainty/verification state;
- reduce inference cadence first on low-change feeds when the CPU budget is saturated, while measuring event delay and missed-event trade-off;
- prohibit cross-camera identity joins and expire raw uploads/evidence according to configured policy.

A suitable formal evaluation would compare (A) fixed cadence with per-frame cues, (B) fixed cadence with temporal confirmation, and (C) BCLTE adaptive cadence, on held-out cameras/videos with event-level annotation. Report event precision/recall/F1, false alerts per camera-hour, missed events, detection delay, source-frame age, FPS, CPU and peak memory. Include bootstrap confidence intervals and camera-wise splits to avoid scene leakage.

**Implementation status:** current prototype has the camera-local temporal heuristics, shared CPU session, human verification, evidence snapshots and retention. Adaptive inference cadence, event-window annotation, calibrated confidence and labelled event evaluation are **not yet implemented**. Therefore BCLTE is a proposed research contribution awaiting prior-art search, implementation and controlled evaluation; the current system must not be presented as having proven novelty or improved accuracy.
